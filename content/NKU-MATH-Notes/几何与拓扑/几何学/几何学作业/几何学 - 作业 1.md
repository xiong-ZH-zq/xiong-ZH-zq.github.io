>Draw a planar picture with at least 3 different non trivial symmetry transformations. Describe all its symmetry.


>[!hint]- 中文翻译
>绘制平面图描绘至少三个非平凡的对称变换，并描述其对称性.


![](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/xiong-ZH-zq/My-PicGO-Img/main/blog/Ex01.png)

The picture above illustrates 3 diverse non trivial symmetry transformations.

1. The first one shows the transformation of reflecting the figure $\triangle ABC$ across the line $\ell$ . This is the reflective symmetry in textbook.
2. The second one shows the transformation of rotating the ellipse $\Gamma$ $360^{\circ}$ around point $O$ . This is the cyclic symmetry.
3. The third one depicts the transformation of translating figure $\Sigma$ to $\Sigma'$ where a fixed point $P$ overlapping its center of gravity.

>[!faq] T2
> Classify all simple quadrilaterals based on their number of symmetry transformations.


>[!hint]- 中文翻译
>将所有简单四边形基于它们对称变换的数量分类.


![](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/xiong-ZH-zq/My-PicGO-Img/main/blog/Ex01-2-1.png)

First, let's denote four vertices with $v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4}$ , and $\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3},\alpha_{4}$ for their interior angle. And we denote four edges with
$$
\begin{cases}
f_{1} = v_{1}v_{2} \\
f_{2} = v_{2}v_{3} \\
f_{3} = v_{3}v_{4} \\
f_{4} = v_{4}v_{1}
\end{cases}
$$
and their length with $l_{1},l_{2},l_{3},l_{4}$ . 

If none of transmation $\varphi(\cdot)$ is symmetric, then we got a quadrilateral with only $1$ symmetry $\mathrm{id}$ .

If a transformation $\varphi(\cdot)$ is symmetric, then $\varphi(v_{1}),\varphi(v_{2}),\varphi(v_{3}),\varphi(v_{4})$ will still be cyclic. Then we need to firstly categorize the question into 3 means:

> ① $\varphi(v_{1})=v_{2}$ .

If $\varphi(v_{4})=v_{1}$ , then
$$
\varphi(v_{2}) = v_{3}, \varphi(v_{3}) = v_{4}
$$
It means we can rotate $v_{1}$ to $v_{2}$ and then get an overlapping shape, which implies $\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{2}$ . And the same goes to $\alpha_{2} = \alpha_{3}$ and $\alpha_{3} = \alpha_{4}$ . Moreover,
$$
\alpha_{1} = \alpha_{2}=\alpha_{3}=\alpha_{4}, l_{1}=l_{2}=l_{3}=l_{4}
$$
Therefore we have a square. It has 8 symmetries.

![](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/xiong-ZH-zq/My-PicGO-Img/main/blog/Ex01-2-2.png)

------

If $\varphi(v_{4})=v_{3}$ , then
$$
\varphi(v_{2}) = v_{1}, \varphi(v_{3}) = v_{4}
$$
This implies:
$$
l_{4} = l_{2}, \alpha_{1} = \alpha_{2}, \alpha_{3} = \alpha_{4}
$$
Thus we have a trapezium. If it is a rectangle, then we have $4$ symmetries. Otherwise, we only have $2$ symmetries.

![](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/xiong-ZH-zq/My-PicGO-Img/main/blog/Ex01-2-3.png)

> ② $\varphi(v_{1})=v_{4}$ .

The same as ① .

> ③ $\varphi(v_{1})=v_{3}$ .

If $\varphi(v_{2})=v_{4}$ , then
$$
\varphi(v_{3}) = \varphi(v_{1}),\varphi(v_{4}) = v_{2}
$$
which implies:
$$
\alpha_{1} = \alpha_{3}, \alpha_{2} = \alpha_{4}, l_{1} = l_{2},l_{3} = l_{4}
$$
Then it is a diamond and it has $4$ symmetries.

![](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/xiong-ZH-zq/My-PicGO-Img/main/blog/Ex01-2-4.png)

In conclusion, we have totally 4 types that has $1,2,4,8$ symmetries. $\square$
